    <p>Vardara Software is a next-generation software development lab. Senior engineers run an AI harness and a software factory across the whole build — spec, code, review, release, and the project management around it. We are nine years old, a team of ten, led by Mahendra Singh, and we work with startups, SMEs and enterprise teams. We are not an agency and we are not a staffing firm. Rails, AWS and React are tools we run. They are not what we are.</p>

    <h2>What does Vardara actually do?</h2>

    <p>Four things, and we say no to work that is not one of them.</p>

    <p><strong>Greenfield AI products.</strong> You bring the thesis, we stand up a real product — prototype in days, paying users in weeks.</p>

    <p><strong>Legacy modernisation.</strong> We read, map and rebuild systems nobody wants to touch, incrementally, with the business logic intact. No big-bang rewrite.</p>

    <p><strong>AI automation and workflows.</strong> Agents and integrations wired into how a business actually runs, measured rather than demoed.</p>

    <p><strong>The software factory.</strong> Our harness as a service — AI across engineering <em>and</em> product management, run by people who own the outcome.</p>

    <h2>What is an AI harness, and why does it matter?</h2>

    <p>Most shops bolt AI onto the coding step and leave the rest of the process alone. Coding was never where the weeks went.</p>

    <p>A harness is the structure around the whole loop: how a spec is written, how work is scoped and re-scoped, how code is reviewed, how tests get generated, how a release goes out and how the thing is watched afterwards. Agents do the volume at every one of those stages. A senior engineer sets the direction and holds the quality bar at each gate, and is accountable for what ships.</p>

    <p>That is why the work compresses. What used to take two quarters lands in weeks, and still holds up in production, because nothing reaches a user without a person having looked at it.</p>

    <p>The tools change constantly. Today the harness runs on Claude, Paperclip, Hermes, n8n, Zapier and MCP, over AWS, React, Rails, Python, Postgres and Terraform. Treat that list as a snapshot, not an identity.</p>

    <h2>How do you know the harness works?</h2>

    <p>Because this site was built with it.</p>

    <p>The homepage, the project brief form and this page were specified, built, reviewed and shipped through the same loop we sell to clients — and every change reached production through a pull request a human merged. We have not instrumented that work in a way we would want quoted back at us, so there is no percentage here. The site is the artefact. You are reading it.</p>

    <h2>Didn't Vardara used to be a Rails shop?</h2>

    <p>Yes. For most of our nine years this page said we were a boutique software company, that Ruby on Rails was our first love and AWS was our home. We said it on the homepage, in our blog posts and in our pitches.</p>

    <p>It was true and it was the wrong thing to sell. The framework was never what made those builds fast — the loop around them was. Selling the framework meant the stack got settled before the problem did, because the stack was also the pitch. We retired that position in October 2026.</p>

    <p>We have left the old arguments up rather than deleting them. Removing a position you no longer hold is cheap; saying which one you held and why you dropped it costs something, and it is the more useful thing for anyone deciding whether to trust us.</p>

    <p>Rails is still a good tool. We still reach for it. It is simply not the headline, and it never should have been.</p>

    <h2 id="mahendra-singh">Who runs Vardara?</h2>

    <p>Mahendra Singh, senior software architect at Vardara Software.</p>

    <p>He spent a decade writing code as a software engineer before any of this was called a harness, and he builds the AWS infrastructure underneath it — for scalability and reliability, which are the two things nobody notices until they are missing. He designs and builds infrastructure, automation and software for enterprise teams, SMEs and startups.</p>

    <p>In October 2026 he approved retiring the Rails-shop positioning Vardara had been known for, and put his own byline on the post making the case against it. He is the named author on the Vardara blog, and nothing on this site goes out without him reading it first.</p>
